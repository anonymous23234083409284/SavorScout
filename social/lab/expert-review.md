# Expert review: what works, what doesn't, and why (Nov 11)

Evidence: all 34 SavorScout TikToks (views, watch time, completion = watch / length, shares per
1,000 views, scripts, frame strips of the best and worst countdowns at 0-10 s), plus Lab v2's
playbook from 76 measured food videos. Confidence: **high** = large, consistent gaps across many
videos; **medium** = consistent but few videos or confounded; **low** = a lead worth testing.

## What works

1. **Countdowns of named schools.** Median 2,230 views and 26% completion, against 261 views / 16%
   for tips and 103 / 7% for general explainers. Tips and explainers got zero shares. **High.**
2. **Groupings people belong to and argue about: conferences and university systems.** They get
   about 5x the shares per view of state groupings (median 15 per 1,000 vs. 2.9) and longer
   watch time (10-13 s vs. 7-10 s). UC (17.9K views, 241 shares) and Big 12 (25 shares per
   1,000) are the clearest cases. States still get views (Penn 5.2K) but rarely get passed
   around. Mechanism: "send this to your friend at another Big 12 school" works when there are
   rivals in the same video; a state list mixes schools nobody compares. **High for shares,
   medium for views.**
3. **Famous schools, and a surprise about one.** The top three all name a household school with
   an unexpected placing in the caption: "UCLA is 5th", "it's not Penn", "Columbia is in New York
   City and it's still not No. 1". Lower performers tease less famous schools (CU Boulder,
   Georgia Southern). **Medium** (it overlaps with point 2).
4. **Holding a third of the video.** The best videos keep about 30-33% completion; the weakest
   countdowns 19-24%. Completion tracks views closely, so the opening is the lever. **Medium-high.**

## What doesn't work

1. **A static opening.** In every countdown, frames at 0, 1, 2.5 and 4 seconds are identical: a
   dark map with four lines of text and a small hint pill. Nothing moves or changes for 4-6
   seconds. In the measured niche, winners cut 2-5 times in the first 3 seconds and put 2-7
   words on the first frame; ours cut zero times and put 12-13. **High** (the gap is large and
   it's where most viewers leave).
2. **A dark first frame.** Our openings sit near the bottom of the niche's brightness range, and
   in the niche model a brighter opening goes with more views (x1.45 per step, likely). **Medium.**
3. **A slow road to the first reveal.** The weaker videos spend 6-10 seconds on the hook and the
   bottom-of-the-list rows (Texas and Ohio are still on the list at 10 s) before the first map.
   The best reach #5 by about 6 s. **Medium.**
4. **Tips and general explainers.** Already dropped. **High.**
5. **Running the same template six times in two days.** The Nov 9-10 state countdowns are
   still young, but they have the lowest shares and are identical in shape. **Low-medium.**

## What to change (in order of expected impact)

1. **Rebuild the first 3 seconds.** A short, large opening line (6-7 words, e.g. "UC campuses,
   ranked by food nearby"), with the full question as the caption; something moving by 1 s
   (a punch-in, dots popping in fast, the count starting); the first reveal by 3-4 s. Test it
   against the current opening. Needs the owner's OK because it moves the full question off the
   first frame.
2. **Pick groupings for sharing.** Conferences, systems, famous-school sets and rivalries first;
   states only when they hold a real rivalry or a famous upset.
3. **Brighten the opening.** A lighter map style or a bright campus or food shot behind the
   opening line.
4. **Speed through the bottom of the list.** Show the low ranks in one fast beat; spend the time
   on the top five.
5. **Lead every caption with the surprise about the most famous school.**
